Comprehensive academic and practical guide to the Vector Space Model, TF-IDF Vectorization, and Cosine Similarity

1 Project Objective & Problem Statement

Standard relational databases rely on exact boolean string matching (e.g. WHERE title LIKE '%Baahubali%'). Such systems fail when users search with fuzzy phrases, describe the plot without recalling the exact title, or search across ambiguous multi-genre concepts.

This college project implements an Intelligent Movie Search and Recommendation System using formal Information Retrieval Systems (IRS) concepts. Specifically focusing on Tollywood (Telugu cinema), the system transforms unstructured textual movie documents into high-dimensional vector spaces, computes geometric angles of relevance, and recommends semantically similar Telugu movies.

2 End-to-End Information Retrieval Pipeline

1. Document Preprocessing Load movies.csv, lowercase, remove punctuation, strip stopwords, and construct composite document tokens (Title × 3, Genre × 3, Category × 2, Director, Cast, Description).
2. TF-IDF Vectorization Compute Term Frequencies ($TF$) and Inverse Document Frequencies ($IDF$) with unigram and bigram tokenization ($N\text{-grams} = (1, 2)$) and sublinear scaling.
3. Vector Space Projection Each movie is projected as an $L_2$-normalized vector in a $V$-dimensional Euclidean space (where $V = {{ stats.vocabulary_size }}$ unique word features).
4. User Query Vectorization When a user submits a search query, it is preprocessed and mapped into the identical $V$-dimensional space using the fitted vocabulary.
5. Cosine Similarity Ranking Calculate dot product between the query vector $\vec{Q}$ and every document vector $\vec{D}_i$. Rank results in descending order of similarity score.
6. Content-Based Recommendation For any selected movie, compute pairwise Cosine Similarity against all other movie vectors, exclude the self-match, prioritize Tollywood titles, and return at least 5 similar recommendations.

3 Understanding TF-IDF in Simple English

TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency. It measures how important a word is to a particular movie within the entire collection:

  • Term Frequency (TF): How frequently a word appears in a specific movie document. If the word "Kattappa" appears multiple times in Baahubali, its TF for that movie is high.
  • Inverse Document Frequency (IDF): How rare or unique a word is across the entire movie dataset. Common words like "the", "movie", or "and" appear in almost every record, so their IDF is nearly zero. Rare discriminative words like "reincarnation" or "cricket" appear in only a few movies, receiving a very high IDF weight.
TF-IDF Mathematical Formula
$$\text{TF-IDF}(t, d, D) = \text{TF}(t, d) \times \text{IDF}(t, D)$$
Where $\text{IDF}(t, D) = \ln\left(\frac{1 + |D|}{1 + \text{DF}(t)}\right) + 1$, and sublinear $\text{TF}(t, d) = 1 + \ln(\text{count}(t, d))$.

4 Understanding Cosine Similarity

In a Vector Space Model, each movie is represented as an arrow (vector) pointing in multi-dimensional space.

Instead of measuring the straight-line Euclidean distance between two vectors (which is biased by document length or description word count), Cosine Similarity measures the cosine of the angle $\theta$ between the two vectors:

  • If two movies discuss identical themes (e.g., period warfare, fantasy kingdoms), their vectors point in nearly the same direction: $\theta \approx 0^\circ \implies \cos(\theta) \approx 1.0$ (High Similarity).
  • If two movies have completely orthogonal content (e.g., cricket drama vs horror black magic), their vectors are perpendicular: $\theta \approx 90^\circ \implies \cos(\theta) \approx 0.0$ (Zero Similarity).
Cosine Similarity Formula
$$\text{Cosine Similarity}(\vec{A}, \vec{B}) = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{\|\vec{A}\| \|\vec{B}\|} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{V} A_i B_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{V} A_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{V} B_i^2}}$$
